## Dominant Narrative Now
Regulatory and infrastructure developments are shaping the US natural gas outlook, with the Mountain Valley pipeline extension advancing and PJM seeing a surge in new power project applications. Storage remains in surplus territory after another strong EIA injection, keeping pressure on prompt pricing. Meanwhile, the anticipated growth in data center load, especially in Texas, signals potential for future demand increases, but near-term sentiment is bearish as Henry Hub futures grind lower. The market is balancing robust storage builds against regulatory-driven infrastructure expansion and evolving power sector demand.

## Top 5 Narratives
### 1. Regulatory and project timing
Regulatory decisions and project timelines are directly influencing gas infrastructure availability and future market balance.

The court's denial of a stay on the Mountain Valley pipeline extension clears a key hurdle for Southeast gas flows, while PJM's influx of project applications points to a wave of new power infrastructure that could reshape regional demand. Data center expansion in Texas is also driving long-term infrastructure planning, though challenges remain. These developments are converging to tighten the outlook for gas infrastructure and repricing, even as near-term market sentiment remains cautious.

Delays or reversals in regulatory approvals would undermine the tightening narrative.

### 2. Regional basis pressure
Regional basis pressures are easing as storage builds and lower flows weigh on spot pricing, particularly in the Northeast and Texas.

Henry Hub futures are under pressure amid expectations of continued storage surplus, and recent cash market action shows mixed to weaker pricing in key regions. Lower net flows and robust injections are loosening regional balances, with basis differentials reflecting the shifting supply-demand landscape. The market is watching for signs of renewed demand or production cuts to reverse this trend.

A sudden uptick in demand or unexpected production outages could quickly tighten regional basis.

### 3. Storage balance and inventory trajectory
US gas storage remains in surplus after another strong injection, reinforcing a loose market balance heading into summer.

The EIA's 79 Bcf injection lifts inventories to 2.142 Tcf, keeping storage above seasonal norms and weighing on prompt prices. Traders are focused on whether this surplus will persist as summer cooling demand ramps up. The current trajectory suggests the market can absorb moderate demand shocks without immediate price support.

Hotter-than-expected weather or supply disruptions could accelerate storage draws and tighten balances.

### 4. Production and supply trend
Production trends are mixed, with some regions holding output flat while others see declines amid weak basin pricing.

Recent reports show Appalachian output at an 11-week low, while other producers maintain steady volumes. Lower production is providing some offset to storage builds, but has not yet tightened the overall market. Traders are monitoring for further supply cuts that could shift the balance.

A rebound in production or sustained flat output would weaken the tightening supply narrative.

### 5. Power burn and demand pull
Power sector demand, led by data center growth and new project applications, is emerging as a key driver of future gas consumption.

PJM's project queue and Texas data center expansion highlight the potential for significant incremental gas demand over the next several years. Utilities are responding with new gas-fired generation proposals, though infrastructure and regulatory challenges remain. While current power burn is not yet shifting balances, the market is increasingly attentive to these long-term demand signals.

Delays in project execution or a shift toward non-gas generation would temper the bullish demand outlook.
