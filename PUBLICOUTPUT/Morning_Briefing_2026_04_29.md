## Top 5 Narratives
### 1. Regulatory and project timing
Regulatory and permitting developments are accelerating the timeline for new gas infrastructure in the Northeast, Southeast, and Midwest. Court skepticism toward environmental challenges and large-scale pipeline proposals are increasing the likelihood of new capacity coming online.

Recent court proceedings have put environmental group challenges on the defensive, while utilities and developers are advancing major pipeline and power projects. CMS Energy’s pipeline proposal could significantly increase regional gas demand, and judicial pushback against stays on projects like Southgate signal regulatory momentum. These developments are converging across multiple regions, suggesting that infrastructure additions could arrive sooner than previously expected.

A successful legal challenge or unexpected regulatory delay would undermine the tightening narrative.

### 2. Regional basis pressure
Regional basis volatility is intensifying as maintenance events and shifting demand patterns disrupt local balances, especially in Texas and the Northeast. Mixed spot pricing and reduced flows are creating opportunities and risks for basis traders.

Waha Hub maintenance is set to reduce Texas flows by 0.5 Bcf/d, tightening supply and potentially widening basis differentials. In the Northeast, spot prices are mixed as higher demand and lower storage injections create localized volatility. These factors are driving divergent price action across regions, with traders needing to monitor both supply disruptions and demand shifts.

A rapid normalization of flows or unexpected demand softness would ease basis pressure.

### 3. Storage balance and inventory trajectory
Late-season cold is curbing US gas storage injections, tightening the storage outlook and raising the risk of price spikes if weather remains volatile. The end of the storage surplus streak is in focus for traders managing inventory risk.

Recent data shows that colder-than-normal temperatures have reduced storage injections, particularly in the Northeast and nationally. This shift is narrowing the storage surplus and increasing sensitivity to further weather-driven demand. If injections remain below expectations, the market could quickly pivot to a tighter balance, amplifying price volatility into the summer.

A return to mild weather or a surge in production would restore storage comfort.

### 4. Production and supply trend
Production trends remain a key watchpoint, with recent maintenance and basin pricing weakness contributing to lower output in Appalachia and Texas. Supply-side developments are adding to regional tightness.

Scheduled maintenance at Waha Hub and weak pricing in Appalachia have pushed production to multi-week lows in some basins. These supply constraints are feeding into regional basis volatility and tightening the overall market balance. Traders should watch for further disruptions or rebounds in output as maintenance concludes.

A swift recovery in production or unexpected supply additions would loosen the market.

### 5. LNG feedgas and terminal pull
LNG terminal activity continues to influence US gas balances, with Gulf Coast feedgas demand and global market dynamics shaping domestic flows. Project milestones and export trends remain critical for forward pricing.

Recent headlines highlight ongoing Gulf Coast LNG feedgas demand and new project developments, such as Louisiana LNG advances. US LNG cargoes are offsetting some global supply losses, keeping domestic balances tighter than they would otherwise be. However, any slowdown in feedgas demand or project delays could quickly shift the outlook.

A drop in LNG feedgas demand or export disruptions would weaken the tightening thesis.
