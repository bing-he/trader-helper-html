## Top 5 Narratives
### 1. Regulatory and project timing
Regulatory and project timing is accelerating infrastructure changes that will tighten the US gas market over the next several years. Recent FIDs and project updates confirm that new supply and export capacity are moving forward, while some regulatory decisions may delay regional gas demand growth.

TC Energy's FID on an Appalachia supply project and NextDecade's confirmation of Rio Grande LNG's 2027 start both reinforce a pipeline of new capacity coming online. ExxonMobil's Golden Pass LNG Train 2 is also on track for end-2026, adding to Gulf Coast export demand. Meanwhile, the Colorado coal plant extension may slow gas demand growth in the Rockies, but does not offset the broader trend of infrastructure-driven tightening. These developments collectively point to a more constrained market balance as new projects reach completion.

Delays or cancellations of major infrastructure projects would undermine the tightening thesis.

### 2. Regional basis pressure
Regional basis pressure is likely to intensify as infrastructure projects shift flows and export pull increases, particularly in the Northeast, Texas, and Midwest.

The confirmation of new pipeline and LNG export projects will alter regional flow patterns and could exacerbate basis volatility, especially as Appalachia gains new egress and Gulf Coast export capacity ramps up. Near-term, lower LNG feedgas demand and the extension of coal generation in Colorado may temporarily ease basis pressure in some regions. However, as these projects come online, traders should anticipate renewed tightening and shifting regional spreads.

If demand growth underperforms or new capacity is delayed, basis pressure could ease instead.

### 3. Storage balance and inventory trajectory
Storage balance is currently loosening as injections outpace expectations and LNG feedgas demand softens, but this may reverse as new export capacity comes online.

Recent declines in LNG feedgas demand and seasonal maintenance at Cameron LNG are contributing to a more comfortable storage trajectory in the near term. However, the medium-term outlook is for increased export pull and infrastructure-driven tightening, which could quickly erode storage buffers. Traders should monitor the timing of project completions and regulatory decisions for signs of a shift back toward tighter storage balances.

Sustained weak export demand or mild weather could keep storage levels elevated longer than expected.

### 4. Production and supply trend
Production and supply trends are currently stable, but new pipeline projects may enable higher output from constrained regions like Appalachia.

With TC Energy's Appalachia project reaching FID, the potential for increased supply into the Midwest is rising, though immediate production signals remain flat. Seasonal factors and maintenance are currently limiting export demand, which may temporarily mask underlying supply growth potential. As new infrastructure comes online, production from key basins could increase, tightening the market balance if demand and exports rise as projected.

If production growth fails to materialize or demand disappoints, supply-driven tightening will not occur.

### 5. Power burn and demand pull
Power burn and gas demand from the power sector are set to remain volatile, with coal plant extensions and renewables integration influencing near-term gas use.

The extension of a Colorado coal plant may dampen regional gas demand growth, while ongoing renewables buildout in other regions could further moderate power burn. However, as data center demand and summer weather risks increase, power-sector gas demand could rebound quickly. Traders should watch for shifts in generation mix and weather-driven demand spikes that could tighten the balance unexpectedly.

A mild summer or further coal and renewables gains could suppress power burn and gas demand.
